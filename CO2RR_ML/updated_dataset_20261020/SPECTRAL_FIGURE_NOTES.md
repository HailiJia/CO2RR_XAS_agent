- Figure 04 now shows one held-out F1 score per species. Labels give the number of unique positive configurations in the 87-structure cohort, including mixtures; those counts are not atom counts or counts of layer spectra. Gray bars and an asterisk flag fewer than five positives. Precision and recall remain available in provisional_best_per_species.csv.
- The replacement spectral overlay is an energy-resolved heatmap using the population-weighted top-two-layer average, normalized per configuration. Each row is the mean spectrum for structures containing a species minus the mean for structures without it. All rows share one signed absorption scale. Red means greater absorption when present; blue means lower.
- The strongest separated local maxima of the RMS contrast across species with at least five positives occur at 8978.8, 8981.8, 8991.2 and 8998.2 eV. Thus roughly 8978–8983 eV is the largest contrast region in this cohort, with additional differences around 8991 and 8998 eV. These energies use the recorded simulation scale.
- H has a pronounced negative contrast in the early region; OH has a positive contrast near 8991 eV. Several carbon-containing species have similar early-positive/later-negative patterns, so a large overall difference does not imply easy separation among those species.
- The separate species panels show the positive-group mean ± one configuration standard deviation and the absent-group mean. The band describes configuration variability, not uncertainty in the mean; the singleton COCHO has no measurable within-group spread.
- These are descriptive full-cohort comparisons, not model importance or validated unique fingerprints. Coverage, substrate structure and coadsorbates are not matched between the groups and can contribute to the contrasts. No energy windows were fed back into model selection, and model scores and folds are unchanged.
